Stop 1 · The factory gate

Australia stopped making cars. It never stopped making dinner.

The trolley starts at the factory gate. Australia's car plants closed and its steel mills shrank. Food factories kept growing, and they now add more value than any other kind of factory.

22%

of all factory value is food.
So what is on the factory floor? Next stop: inside.

Stop 2 · On the factory floor

Meat makes the money, but bakeries make the jobs

Food manufacturing is really eight industries sharing one floor. Some earn the most, others employ the most people. Each keeps the same colour from here on.

Stop 3 · Back at the farm gate

The factory gate is never far from the farm gate

Milk, grapes and meat are processed close to where they grow. That makes food factories vital to country towns, and some states depend on them far more than others.

$41.6 billion

of food was sold overseas last year.
Where did it go? Next stop: the port.

Stop 4 · At the port

Much of what Australia makes is eaten overseas

Food exports grew from $24.0 billion to $41.6 billion, a rise of three quarters in nine years. Meat leads the way.

$8.3 billion

came from the United States alone.
And what do Australians buy themselves? Next stop: the checkout.

Stop 5 · At the checkout

What Australians buy is changing what factories make

In five years Australians bought more snacks and plant-based foods. They bought less fruit, sugar and dairy.

31%

more plant-based milk and meat per person.
So what is the whole story? Last stop: the receipt.

What to remember

Mostly, Australia still makes food

Food is now Australia's largest manufacturing industry. It keeps country towns working, from Gippsland to the Riverland. It feeds shoppers here and buyers across the world. Next time you fill a trolley, think of the factory behind it.

About this story

What: the data

Seven public sources, cleaned with one Python script into small CSV files. Financial years run from July to June, so "2025" means July 2024 to June 2025. Dollar values are not adjusted for inflation. Dairy and wine region positions are approximate region centres. Export figures for the year to June 2026 are preliminary and are not shown.

Why: the purpose

Many Australians think manufacturing faded with the car plants. This story shows the opposite for food, and follows it from the factory floor to the shopping trolley for a general Australian audience.

How: the design

Each food industry keeps one colour on every chart. Teal means more and dark grey means fewer. The palette avoids red and green pairs. Maps use equal-area projections. Interaction follows the overview, zoom, filter, details order.
